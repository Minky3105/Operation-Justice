Project Balloon God is an experimental and highly controversial initiative designed to explore extreme life-dependence scenarios. In this experiment, a human subject is physically connected to a balloon via an electrical mechanism that serves as the conduit for the life-link. The central premise is that the subject's survival becomes entirely dependent on the balloon remaining airborne. If the balloon touches the ground, the mechanism triggers an immediate fatal consequence for the subject, making the ground-contact event the sole determinant of mortality.

Remarkably, while the balloon is airborne and connected, the subject attains a form of conditional invulnerability. This connection effectively renders all other threats to life — including falls from great heights, physical trauma such as stabbing, natural aging processes, and other conventional dangers — completely ineffective. The subject cannot die from any cause other than the balloon touching the ground, creating a scenario in which life and death are uniquely mediated by the status of the airborne object.

The experiment raises profound questions regarding the nature of dependency, risk, and the perception of safety. It transforms the mundane act of keeping a balloon afloat into a life-or-death responsibility, making the subject's entire existence contingent on a single, external object. In essence, Project Balloon God constructs a world in microcosm where human survival is no longer determined by natural processes or conventional hazards, but by a fragile tether between human and balloon.
